GitHub MFA Requirement Disabled

edit

Detects when the two-factor authentication (2FA) requirement is disabled for a GitHub organization or enterprise. Removing the requirement downgrades every member in scope to password-only authentication and allows new members to be added without 2FA. Following an account takeover, an adversary may disable the requirement to preserve password-based access, reduce friction for enrolling attacker-controlled accounts, and lower the organization’s overall account-takeover resistance, making this a high-impact identity-control change.

Investigating GitHub MFA Requirement Disabled

This rule triggers on business.disable_two_factor_requirement and org.disable_two_factor_requirement, which turn off the enforced two-factor authentication (2FA) requirement for an entire GitHub enterprise or organization. Removing the requirement downgrades every member in scope to password-only authentication and permits new members to be invited without 2FA. An adversary who has taken over an owner or enterprise-admin account may disable the requirement to preserve password-based access, reduce friction for adding attacker-controlled accounts, and lower the organization’s account-takeover resistance before pursuing persistence, privilege escalation, or data theft.

Possible investigation steps

  • Identify the actor from user.name, github.actor_ip, github.user_agent, and github.programmatic_access_type, and confirm whether they legitimately hold organization owner or enterprise admin privileges.
  • Determine the scope of the change from event.action (org.disable_two_factor_requirement vs business.disable_two_factor_requirement) and the affected github.org/github.business, and verify whether it was approved in change-management records.
  • Review authentication context for the actor around the event time for signs of takeover, such as sign-ins from new github.actor_ip addresses, unfamiliar user agents, or token-based access (github.programmatic_access_type) in place of normal interactive activity.
  • Correlate for other high-impact identity changes near the same timestamp, including owner or member additions, PAT or OAuth app grants, SSH/GPG key additions, SSO/SAML changes, and IP allow-list modifications.
  • If the change is unauthorized, re-enable the 2FA requirement before continuing the investigation to limit exposure.

False positive analysis

  • A documented break-glass procedure during an identity-provider or 2FA outage may require temporarily disabling the requirement; this should be rare, ticketed, time-bounded, and followed by immediate re-enablement.
  • A planned governance or migration change (for example, consolidating organizations or adjusting enrollment policy) performed by a known administrator can generate this event as expected activity.

Response and remediation

  • Re-enable the required 2FA setting for the affected organization or enterprise as soon as the change is confirmed unauthorized.
  • Force sign-out of active sessions and audit the member list for accounts not enrolled in 2FA, requiring enrollment or suspending them.
  • Rotate credentials and tokens created or used after the requirement was disabled, including PATs, OAuth app grants, and SSH keys.
  • Review org and enterprise audit logs for other changes made by the actor after the disable event, and revert any unauthorized modifications.
  • Escalate to incident response if the actor is unknown or unauthorized, or if the disable event is accompanied by new owner additions, token grants, or sign-ins from unfamiliar infrastructure.
  • Harden by enforcing 2FA at the enterprise level, restricting who can change organization security settings, and alerting on future changes to the 2FA requirement.

Rule query

edit
from logs-github.audit-* metadata _id, _index, _version
| where
  event.module == "github" and data_stream.dataset == "github.audit"
    and event.action in ("business.disable_two_factor_requirement", "org.disable_two_factor_requirement")
| keep
    _id,
    _index,
    _version,
    @timestamp,
    event.action,
    user.name,
    github.org,
    github.repo,
    github.actor_ip,
    github.hashed_token,
    github.programmatic_access_type,
    github.user_agent,
    github.business
